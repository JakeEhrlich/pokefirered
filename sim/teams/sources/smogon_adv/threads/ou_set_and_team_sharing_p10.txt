"COTH" - Explanation & Replays
Click Sprites for PokePaste

I've been wanting to remake this post for a while now - I had made a very short write-up for this team when I started using it in July 2025, but since playing tours I tried to cut down on/remove my forum posting for privacy. However, this team has been spammed like crazy recently (both on ladder and in tournament) to the point where it doesn't matter whether it's posted and explained publicly or not. If that's the case, I think it absolutely deserves a formal write-up. It's my favorite team I've ever made and has really caught much more public wind this year than I expected, so it feels kind of silly for it go undocumented. I also hope that by posting/archiving the team, some of the spamming will die down a bit, because in reality it probably is a bit overused and misplayed. But I think it's also quite misunderstood, which is another reason to write.

For some context before the main post though: between ROAPL, ADV Cup, ADVGC, ADVPL, etc, I managed to go 22-4 with this team in tournament so far in 2026, and I also got a super lucky high ladder run to 1985 ELO in June of this year, even when most opponents already know that I spam this team. I've posted all those tournament replays as well as some of the high ladder replays at the bottom of the post. Maybe those results don't mean anything to some people, but I'm personally very happy with how it's done for me. Of course, tons of people have used this exact 6 before with various movesets, so it's very far from original, but nowadays I do probably use it the most (possibly too much...).

Background & Motivation.

The team was first and foremost designed to excel at "noobfarming" on ladder.

I guess it's no secret that I'd been majorly addicted to laddering since 2023 until this year, and in past years had always been trying to "solve it", meaning, I wanted to find a team that can consistently beat all teams you could potentially face on ladder at any moment in time (which is essentially every single possible combination of Pokemon + any crazy set of moves you could imagine). This one is closer to achieving that than any of my other ladder teams from 2023 - 2025 by far.

Even if you think it's dumb, just subscribe to the following idea for the time-being: in the above laddering context (deliberately trying to build for 2000 ELO with a single team), spikes are highly desirable, or perhaps simply essential. It's pretty well understood that spikes, sand, and maybe trappers, make climbing the easiest due to their consistency in the context of facing highly random teams/compositions. These strategies (especially spikes) are often harder for the inexperienced low-ladderers to understand: a lot of them either choose to use teams that are weak to spikes+sand without considering their impact, and/or don't know how to play around them (though, this is becoming less true). It just tremendously simplifies the matchups when you face such extremely random compositions on the ladder, simple as that. This paragraph could be an entire post in its own right, and there's other desirable traits for such a team to consider, so I'll just stop there for now and ask the reader to buy in for a moment.

Pretend that you want a team that has most or all of the below elements:

1. Spikes
2. Tyranitar
3. Blissey+Spin
4. Rock Resist
5. The move Calm Mind OR Gengar
6. One of Salamence, Aerodactyl, Zapdos, Moltres/Charizard
7. An offensive Pokemon with a high potential for luck or "hax" (such as Aerodactyl rock slide, Metagross meteor mash)

Again, not at all saying you have to have all of these things in a good ladder team, it's indeed very hard to fit all those, and most great teams lack at least 1-2 of them. But a lot of historically really successful ladder teams do have most of these traits - Skarpherim's "Sapphire Beach" 2036 ELO team has all of them, El Classico and Badummy's team has all but (3), etc. By that I really just mean, many very very strong players have favored these traits in their chosen teams for very high ladder runs.

The above list of traits for a team is basically the "Big5" mold. Big5 used to be considered the most standard archetype due to its neutrality (not really the case anymore), and close to neutral is basically what we are going for on ladder. However, it's not possible to fit all of them - the question is, which ones will you drop/compress? This is the question that motivates a lot of spikes teams. Maybe you compress (3) and (5) with CM Bliss. Maybe you save space fitting (3) and (1) using Forretress. Maybe you use Aerodactyl to have (6) and (7) in one slot. I guess I am wagering that compressing Spikes+Spin in this context is very good, but that's again a conversation for a separate post. I wanted to find a team that has basically all of these traits, especially (7), because in my opinion it's quite essential for stringing together a ton of games (a massive luck streak is necessary to an extent for a huge ladder run). It does really sound very stupid, but I insist (7) is a super valuable trait for a team to have in this context.

All of that laddering background stuff said (which, again, could be a whole separate post entirely), I'll talk about the game-plan I decided on, motivated by the above list of properties I wanted for my team. Consider the following Jirachi spread, which the whole team is built around, and is motivated by the above ideas.

Pokemon.

Jirachi @ Leftovers
Ability: Serene Grace
EVs: 252 HP / 88 SpA / 168 Spe
Timid Nature
IVs: 0 Atk
- Thunderbolt
- Ice Punch
- Substitute
- Calm Mind

The centerpiece to the team. After getting smashed by the top players using this on ladder in 2024, and seeing their success with it, I realized that theoretically this thing is outstanding for laddering. It appreciates/satisfies almost all of the above traits in a totally unique way: it has natural synergy with spikes, really wants to have sand, gives you a backup rock resist, is basically the scariest calm minder, and worst of all ... potentially most dishonorable of all ... you always have a 20% to freeze. It matchup divides basically all fat teams, plays around Dugtrio with substitute, abuses sand to beat Blissey, and even worse, it can still game-rob faster paced teams. Not to mention the obvious, which is that BoltBeam coverage is great with spikes. Very strong and basically unparalleled traits in a "noobfarming" context, especially because it also really badly punishes players who don't know how to beat it in a way that other Pokemon can't replicate. It also has 100% accurate moves, and it's very slippery by nature, so it converts games very quickly and easily if given the right support. But that's the issue - it's surprisingly hard to build around. Nearly impossible, maybe. The goal for the rest of the team attempts to shoot the gap between fitting as many of the above traits as possible while supporting Jirachi. In order to abuse it's traits (sand immunity, hax) to the full extent, it's natural to ask for spin support, and even wish support. Wish allows Jirachi to spam Substitute multiple times per game if needed, instead of forcing the player to go all in with it at the perfect moment. This way, you can roll paras/freezes a huge amount of times in the longer games if you need to. So, the rest of the team will focus on fitting those slots instead of purely building around offensive synergy with it, especially since I also want to prioritize fitting a Blissey.

Blissey @ Leftovers
Ability: Natural Cure
EVs: 224 HP / 252 Def / 32 Spe
Bold Nature
IVs: 0 Atk
- Seismic Toss
- Thunder Wave
- Soft-Boiled
- Wish

As mentioned above, for the goal of this team, it's desirable to have a Blissey and also Wish support. So, Wish Blissey. Additionally, Bold with TWave is unkillable for a huge amount of teams. It's just an extremely strong defensive Pokemon in Gen3 due to the power level, and synergizes well with Spikes. Most importantly, the use of Wish enables positioning for Jirachi and Forretress in long games: if needed, you can last a bit longer in the Spikes war by wishing up Forre, or you can wish up Jirachi after spinning to go in for a second or even third time with it. Helps absorb status, paralyzes enemies for sub/sand abuse, etc. And again, if playing vs less experienced players on ladder, Blissey's variance reduction is super helpful. I think this set fits very naturally alongside Jirachi and Forretress. In general, I've seen Wish+TWave gaining a lot of traction. I call this moveset "God" Bliss. You could try to use Toxic, since Blissey can lure Pert/Tar for Jirachi, but I find that this moveset has the better overall matchups, which again, in the ladder context is the main goal. I suppose that by choosing to use Blissey, it takes the team in a more defensive direction - but I'd argue the use of this particular moveset does not lock the team into that style of play. It acts as a universal pivot - you can live basically anything and pass a Wish. It's useful in the face of those momentum based offenses too, so indeed you do have options in both fast and slow games with this moveset. And of course, Wish can be passed to the other members too, enabling more exotic win paths sometimes. Very cool set.

Forretress @ Leftovers
Ability: Sturdy
EVs: 248 HP / 8 Def / 252 SpD
Sassy Nature
IVs: 30 SpD / 30 Spe
- Hidden Power [Bug]
- Spikes
- Rapid Spin
- Zap Cannon / Explosion

It's actually a very very good partner for SubCM Jirachi in particular. If you investigate the other possible teams that use the above Jirachi spread, you'll find that the best versions either drop Swampert for Claydol, or drop Blissey+Spin entirely. I do not wish to drop Blissey on ladder due to its defensive consistency in the face of extreme variance vs inexperienced players, but also want Swampert and TTar: and so, the best way to fix this slot issue is to just use Forretress. Here is the super important thing to understand about my version of this team: this team should NOT be viewed as a "Forre Balance" just because I am using a Forretress. I guess it is a "balance", but it's more about going all in to support Jirachi while still building a fundamentally solid team, and Forretress just happens to be useful for that. This is more accurate than pinning the team as a "Forre Team". The moves are straight forward, except that you should strongly consider using Zap Cannon, as it's well known that even if you use Pursuit TTar, your forretress team will struggle with Gengar. Zap Cannon owns Gar on the switch, and also really irritates Skarmory. It's a great tool to make the Skarm+Gar matchup more playable. Bug/Zap Cannon in general can irritate all spikers, spinners, and spinblockers. Together with the extremely valuable compression, the team will at least have a hand in the spikes war, while not relying on winning it (because we have the demonic Jirachi set), as Forretress is not that type of Pokemon. I'd maybe argue after all my time with these "Ladder teams" and Big5 variants, that compressing the Spike+Spin role is probably underrated for laddering, because in matchups where the opponent brings no spikes, you've saved an entire slot, and due to the quite often large skill gap on ladder, playing Forre Spikes is not really a problem. It's not as good vs experienced players obviously, but again, teambuilding is in large part about context/goal, and the goal of this team is to farm on ladder. Forretress is actually really good for that too.

Tyranitar @ Leftovers
Ability: Sand Stream
EVs: 212 HP / 252 SpA / 44 Spe
Quiet Nature
- Crunch
- Brick Break  / Counter
- Flamethrower / Fire Blast
- Pursuit

Not much to say. Sand support for Jirachi, sand in general on ladder, pursuit support to enable both Forretress and Mono Wish Blissey. The team is looking cooked vs Snorlax and also can't directly pressure opposing Blissey - so Brick Break is helpful. It also allows our set to trade into opposing TTar if needed, which also supports Jirachi. One thing I'll mention is that it'd be interesting to try to use a physical TTar, which would have more direct offensive synergy with Jirachi, and then maybe use Ghost Forretress. The issue is you also really would like that Blissey set. So, I think just a standard Pursuit TTar ends up being better. But it's an interesting question that I'd like to explore more. I think the recent TTar/Forre teams that drop Pursuit are probably quite good. One important note is that this team ends up forfeiting to lead dug weather clear into Suicune/Snorlax types of teams, so you may want to slot counter if leading with TTar, or you can just lead Salamence and outplay, but it's still a very weak matchup. If you use Counter for lead Dug, use max HP and defense EV's, of course.

Swampert @ Leftovers
Ability: Torrent
EVs: 248 HP / 228 Def / 32 Spe
Bold Nature
IVs: 0 Atk
- Surf
- Toxic
- Protect
- Roar

The modern standard defensive Swampert. Fits great alongside TTar and Forre. Roar is necessary because we don't have a Skarmory. This slot is more deliberately defensive than the others, in that it's kinda just necessary to have it on this composition. You can ask maybe should we have some offensive set that can lure Jirachi's checks but not really. Again, this set is really awesome to ladder with. There are lots of status/TSS weak offenses ladderers use that will struggle vs this. Not much to say since it's very standard, but just pretty much the only conclusion for a physical wall alongside Jirachi, TTar, and Forretress (can't really be metagross or flygon, etc). Something to note: I do not attempt to use Aroma Bliss or Refresh Pert on this team. This is because the team is not intending to last forever in long games, it's just intending to win with Jirachi within ~100-200 turns if needed, not rest/aroma up for 300+. The point is that you end up with enough offensive tempo with Jirachi vs those very slow paced teams, and in the faster paced games, you love to have ProTox pert.

Salamence @ Leftovers
Ability: Intimidate
EVs: 4 Atk / 252 SpA / 252 Spe
Naive Nature
- Dragon Claw
- Brick Break
- Flamethrower / Fire Blast
- Hidden Power [Grass]

The last slot to fit is a bird. In terms of breaking synergy for Jirachi, actually it's maybe the worst in a sense - Aero could lure Pert/Meta better, or perhaps Molt/Zard could sometimes lure a TTar. But again, I kind of argue that in this context it's better to slot the "generally good" fit, as oppose to thinking about direct offensive synergy for Jirachi. The goal as stated previously, is to Spin+Wish for it, and fit the other strong ladder traits. Salamence as the bird fits great for all of those other reasons. Intimidate+Fighting resist is excellent on ladder; it also destroys lots of Magneton offenses, it also synergizes great defensive with Bold Blissey (anything that gets intimidated is basically then blanked by Bold Bliss). Also, while not providing direct overload synergy for Jirachi, it can matchup divide for it: in matchups where Salamence is blanked by say MiloDol or RestZap, Jirachi typically can break those teams through Spin+Wish support. For faster paced teams Salamence + 1 Spike is often nice. +Speed nature is mandatory here in my opinion.

Matchups & Conclusion.

To summarize: this team was designed primarily for farming ladder ELO, by including assumptions about skill-gaps and random "Jerry" 6's on ladder into the building process, focusing on supporting SubCM Jirachi as the primary goal of the team instead of over-relying on Forretress to win the spikes war (again, note that the team is far from perfect defensively). Survive long enough in the fat matchup to win with Jirachi, and use classic TSS elements to defend vs generic or "random 6" sand/spike weak ladder offenses. In the end, you get a very consistent and quite flexible team with an emphasis on passive damage and only 1 real missing move (Toxic), to minimize randomness in the face of weaker opponents (the main reason to use flamethrower here). While it is a "balance", hopefully I've convinced you that it plays more like a sort of bulky offense (see replays) with this choice of movesets. But fine, in a way, this team is a huge balancing act. That's sometimes not desirable, but in the right setting/context or vs. the right opponent, having both defensive and offensive options in game can still be very strong in my opinion, even in the hyper polarized modern era.

In terms of tournaments, while I think it's just a generally really sound set of 6 with strong Pokemon, it's definitely not the sort of thing you should use if you're not practiced with it, or if you face someone better than you most of the time (I did manage to steal some wins off great players with it though, so it doesn't have to be just for comfort/ladder). However, I would argue it still has clear use cases. Historically, the Jirachi is chosen to be Defensive with Toxic, but in this version, you have an excellent wincon vs lots of fat teams - especially dugless stalls. I would argue the matchup is good in those cases. In most other cases, it's closer to neutral or on paper perhaps slightly negative across the board - but often that's the case with teams that strive to be close to all around neutral (which, isn't exactly possible anyways). But again, the one really rough matchup for it is dugtrio weather clear stuff, such as Dugtrio+Weather Clear Claydol+Snorlax. What can you do, basically all teams will have a weakness. But even then, this Jirachi set can always get you...

Modest Nature

- Thunderbolt
- Hidden Power [Grass]
- Calm Mind

Hey! Today i bring a cool little Raikou set that might interest some people!

116 defense EVs mean that the standard bulky attacker Metagross does a max of 94.7% damage with Earthquake, while full 252+ atk metagross also STILL doesn't ohko with max 99.4 or .6 % damage! Tho that one u can die to sand after, still it's fine.

The beauty of this spread is that it keeps Raikou speedier than base 100s and pretty powerful, while punishing people who use their Gross as an offensive Raikou check. Fill the last move as you see the need to. Crunch for Gar, Roar for other CMers and Stuff, etc. Raikou is very customizable and that's another beauty of this mon.

Cool set! Just wanted to chime in and point out that somehow the universe perfectly aligns for that agilgross eq benchmark to also be the benchmark for taking less than 25% from defensive rachi body slam:

Here are my thoughts. I’m just theorizing here - these might not work in practice so test them out.

Glalie over Articuno - if your goal is to suicide into tar and meta, doesn’t glalie accomplish the same thing? Boom helps set dug up for a trap more easily, and so does the one spike you’ll occasionally set up.

Baton pass over hp grass on Celebi - I like how celebi pressures things into dug range with leech seed. Why not expand on that and try to set up dug traps more reliably? Grass doesn’t feel necessary here since you have other methods of pressuring ttar, and you can just bp into your own dug against other dugtrio. I guess starmie is problematic, though.

Zapdos over Articuno - this will make your team play differently of course, but I think having another option to check special attackers like zard zap and mie will really help the team out. I like using 2 special checks on every team in general, even blissey teams. Your team has some awkward pacing because you start really aggressive trading 1 for 1 and then settle into a stall game. If the trade doesn’t work out, articuno is kinda just a sitting duck that won’t hit the field again. Glalie addresses this by playing proactively; zapdos addresses this by being more useful as a pivot and checking more things defensively.

Blissey over Celebi - probably too lax weak, but it’ll help your offmie woes. Something to think about.

Roar on Suicune - this will help you against lax and does well enough vs mence. Will help you against boost sweepers in general. I think it’s an upgrade, but maybe I’m wrong.

As far as optimizing these exact 6 mons, I think you’re mostly there outside of potential tweaks to cune and celebi I recommended above. Heal bell is probably worth considering over toxic on articuno, but I like the offensive evs and lead lines as they are.

I think the tar version is not good. Too weak to zapdos and offensive waters for a team that wants to play slowly.

Just a few things to think about. Hope this helped you in some way!

Background & Motivation.

The team was first and foremost designed to excel at "noobfarming" on ladder.

I guess it's no secret that I'd been majorly addicted to laddering since 2023 until this year, and in past years had always been trying to "solve it", meaning, I wanted to find a team that can consistently beat all teams you could potentially face on ladder at any moment in time (which is essentially every single possible combination of Pokemon + any crazy set of moves you could imagine). This one is closer to achieving that than any of my other ladder teams from 2023 - 2025 by far.

Even if you think it's dumb, just subscribe to the following idea for the time-being: in the above laddering context (deliberately trying to build for 2000 ELO with a single team), spikes are highly desirable, or perhaps simply essential. It's pretty well understood that spikes, sand, and maybe trappers, make climbing the easiest due to their consistency in the context of facing highly random teams/compositions. These strategies (especially spikes) are often harder for the inexperienced low-ladderers to understand: a lot of them either choose to use teams that are weak to spikes+sand without considering their impact, and/or don't know how to play around them (though, this is becoming less true). It just tremendously simplifies the matchups when you face such extremely random compositions on the ladder, simple as that. This paragraph could be an entire post in its own right, and there's other desirable traits for such a team to consider, so I'll just stop there for now and ask the reader to buy in for a moment.

Pretend that you want a team that has most or all of the below elements:

1. Spikes
2. Tyranitar
3. Blissey+Spin
4. Rock Resist
5. The move Calm Mind OR Gengar
6. One of Salamence, Aerodactyl, Zapdos, Moltres/Charizard
7. An offensive Pokemon with a high potential for luck or "hax" (such as Aerodactyl rock slide, Metagross meteor mash)

Again, not at all saying you have to have all of these things in a good ladder team, it's indeed very hard to fit all those, and most great teams lack at least 1-2 of them. But a lot of historically really successful ladder teams do have most of these traits - Skarpherim's "Sapphire Beach" 2036 ELO team has all of them, El Classico and Badummy's team has all but (3), etc. By that I really just mean, many very very strong players have favored these traits in their chosen teams for very high ladder runs.

The above list of traits for a team is basically the "Big5" mold. Big5 used to be considered the most standard archetype due to its neutrality (not really the case anymore), and close to neutral is basically what we are going for on ladder. However, it's not possible to fit all of them - the question is, which ones will you drop/compress? This is the question that motivates a lot of spikes teams. Maybe you compress (3) and (5) with CM Bliss. Maybe you save space fitting (3) and (1) using Forretress. Maybe you use Aerodactyl to have (6) and (7) in one slot. I guess I am wagering that compressing Spikes+Spin in this context is very good, but that's again a conversation for a separate post. I wanted to find a team that has basically all of these traits, especially (7), because in my opinion it's quite essential for stringing together a ton of games (a massive luck streak is necessary to an extent for a huge ladder run). It does really sound very stupid, but I insist (7) is a super valuable trait for a team to have in this context.

All of that laddering background stuff said (which, again, could be a whole separate post entirely), I'll talk about the game-plan I decided on, motivated by the above list of properties I wanted for my team. Consider the following Jirachi spread, which the whole team is built around, and is motivated by the above ideas.

Jirachi @ Leftovers

EVs: 252 HP / 88 SpA / 168 Spe
Timid Nature
IVs: 0 Atk

The centerpiece to the team. After getting smashed by the top players using this on ladder in 2024, and seeing their success with it, I realized that theoretically this thing is outstanding for laddering. It appreciates/satisfies almost all of the above traits in a totally unique way: it has natural synergy with spikes, really wants to have sand, gives you a backup rock resist, is basically the scariest calm minder, and worst of all ... potentially most dishonorable of all ... you always have a 20% to freeze. It matchup divides basically all fat teams, plays around Dugtrio with substitute, abuses sand to beat Blissey, and even worse, it can still game-rob faster paced teams. Not to mention the obvious, which is that BoltBeam coverage is great with spikes. Very strong and basically unparalleled traits in a "noobfarming" context, especially because it also really badly punishes players who don't know how to beat it in a way that other Pokemon can't replicate. It also has 100% accurate moves, and it's very slippery by nature, so it converts games very quickly and easily if given the right support. But that's the issue - it's surprisingly hard to build around. Nearly impossible, maybe. The goal for the rest of the team attempts to shoot the gap between fitting as many of the above traits as possible while supporting Jirachi. In order to abuse it's traits (sand immunity, hax) to the full extent, it's natural to ask for spin support, and even wish support. Wish allows Jirachi to spam Substitute multiple times per game if needed, instead of forcing the player to go all in with it at the perfect moment. This way, you can roll paras/freezes a huge amount of times in the longer games if you need to. So, the rest of the team will focus on fitting those slots instead of purely building around offensive synergy with it, especially since I also want to prioritize fitting a Blissey.

It's not as good vs experienced players obviously, but again, teambuilding is in large part about context/goal, and the goal of this team is to farm on ladder. Forretress is actually really good for that too.

Matchups & Conclusion.

To summarize: this team was designed primarily for farming ladder ELO, by including assumptions about skill-gaps and random "Jerry" 6's on ladder into the building process, focusing on supporting SubCM Jirachi as the primary goal of the team instead of over-relying on Forretress to win the spikes war (again, note that the team is far from perfect defensively). Survive long enough in the fat matchup to win with Jirachi, and use classic TSS elements to defend vs generic or "random 6" sand/spike weak ladder offenses. In the end, you get a very consistent and quite flexible team with an emphasis on passive damage and only 1 real missing move (Toxic), to minimize randomness in the face of weaker opponents (the main reason to use flamethrower here). While it is a "balance", hopefully I've convinced you that it plays more like a sort of bulky offense (see replays) with this choice of movesets. But fine, in a way, this team is a huge balancing act. That's sometimes not desirable, but in the right setting/context or vs. the right opponent, having both defensive and offensive options in game can still be very strong in my opinion, even in the hyper polarized modern era.

In terms of tournaments, while I think it's just a generally really sound set of 6 with strong Pokemon, it's definitely not the sort of thing you should use if you're not practiced with it, or if you face someone better than you most of the time (I did manage to steal some wins off great players with it though, so it doesn't have to be just for comfort/ladder). However, I would argue it still has clear use cases. Historically, the Jirachi is chosen to be Defensive with Toxic, but in this version, you have an excellent wincon vs lots of fat teams - especially dugless stalls. I would argue the matchup is good in those cases. In most other cases, it's closer to neutral or on paper perhaps slightly negative across the board - but often that's the case with teams that strive to be close to all around neutral (which, isn't exactly possible anyways). But again, the one really rough matchup for it is dugtrio weather clear stuff, such as Dugtrio+Weather Clear Claydol+Snorlax. What can you do, basically all teams will have a weakness. But even then, this Jirachi set can always get you...
